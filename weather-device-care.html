GUIDE 01

Hot Weather & Cooling

High temperatures can affect batteries, performance and the safe operation of electronics.

Warning: Do not force-cool a hot device.

Avoid placing hot electronics in a refrigerator, freezer or direct contact with ice. Sudden temperature changes can cause condensation.

Preparation checklist

  1. Keep phones, laptops and other electronics out of direct sunlight.
  2. Place equipment on a firm surface that does not block its ventilation openings.
  3. Keep fans and ventilation areas free from dust and obstructions, following the manufacturer's instructions.
  4. Keep refrigerators away from excessive heat where practical and allow the required ventilation clearance.
  5. Reduce demanding tasks on a device that is unusually hot and allow it to cool naturally.

If a device overheats

  1. Stop using the device if it becomes unusually hot or displays a temperature warning.
  2. If it is safe and there are no signs of battery damage, disconnect its charger and switch it off according to the manufacturer's instructions.
  3. Move away from direct sunlight and allow the device to cool naturally in a dry, ventilated place.
  4. Do not use or charge a device with a swollen battery, smoke, a burning smell or visible damage.
When to contact a technician

Seek professional help if overheating happens repeatedly, the battery swells, the device shuts down unexpectedly or there is a burning smell.

GUIDE 02

Heavy Rain & Flood Safety

Water can cause electric shock, short circuits and damage to electronic components.

Never enter floodwater to reach electrical equipment.

Floodwater may be electrically energised. Do not touch wet plugs, switches, appliances or electrical cables.

Prepare before heavy rain

  1. Keep portable electronics, chargers and power banks in a dry location above expected flood levels.
  2. Back up important files and photographs to a secure location.
  3. Keep devices away from open windows, leaking roofs and areas where water may collect.
  4. Follow local emergency instructions if flooding threatens your home.

If a device gets wet

  1. Do not touch the device if it is connected to mains electricity or surrounded by floodwater.
  2. Do not plug in, charge or switch on a wet device.
  3. If the equipment is connected to the mains, keep away and have the supply isolated by a qualified person when needed.
  4. Do not use a hairdryer, oven or other heat source to dry the equipment.
  5. Arrange professional inspection before using mains-powered equipment exposed to water.
When to contact a technician

Contact a qualified repair provider if a phone, laptop or appliance has been exposed to water. Floodwater exposure needs particular caution because contamination and hidden electrical damage may remain.

GUIDE 03

Power Cuts & Power Restoration

Planned preparation can reduce data loss and help protect electronics during outages.

Warning: Never handle exposed wiring.

Do not attempt to repair electrical outlets, breakers, generators or internal appliance wiring.

Before an outage

  1. Save your work regularly and back up important files.
  2. Charge essential portable devices while the power supply is stable.
  3. Use suitable, certified surge protection where appropriate for your equipment.
  4. Follow the manufacturer's instructions for refrigerators, battery backups and other appliances.

When electricity returns

  1. Wait for the supply to become stable before reconnecting sensitive equipment if outages are accompanied by repeated flickering or abnormal voltage.
  2. Reconnect devices according to their manufacturers' instructions.
  3. Check for unusual sounds, burning smells, smoke or visible damage without touching unsafe equipment.
  4. If a circuit breaker repeatedly trips, do not keep resetting it; contact a qualified electrician.
When to contact a technician

Get professional help if equipment fails to start after an outage, repeatedly loses power, produces a burning smell or shows signs of electrical damage.

GUIDE 04

Lightning & Electrical Safety

Thunderstorms can create risks for people, electrical systems and connected equipment.

During a thunderstorm, avoid electrical hazards.

Do not handle outdoor cables, damaged power lines or electrical equipment in wet areas. Follow local emergency guidance.

Before a storm

  1. Back up important data on computers and phones.
  2. Use suitable surge protection installed according to the manufacturer's guidance.
  3. Ask a qualified electrician about appropriate building-level surge protection if your property needs it.
  4. Keep chargers and electrical equipment away from water and damp surfaces.

During and after a storm

  1. Stay indoors in a substantial building when lightning is nearby.
  2. Avoid corded electrical equipment and plumbing during an active thunderstorm.
  3. Do not approach fallen power lines, even if they appear inactive.
  4. After the storm, check equipment visually for damage without opening it.
When to contact a technician

Contact a qualified electrician or repair professional if equipment is damaged after a lightning event, a protective device repeatedly trips or there are signs of burning or arcing.

GUIDE 05

Water Shortages

Water-dependent appliances need an appropriate supply and safe operating conditions.

Do not run appliances outside their operating requirements.

Low or interrupted water supply can affect washing machines and other water-dependent equipment. Follow the manufacturer's guidance.

Practical preparation

  1. Check the water supply before starting a washing machine or other water-dependent appliance.
  2. Follow the manufacturer's instructions if water pressure is low or supply stops during a cycle.
  3. Do not bypass water-level sensors, safety interlocks or appliance controls.
  4. Keep electrical connections and nearby sockets dry when handling water containers.
  5. Store water safely in suitable containers away from electrical equipment.

If the water supply stops

  1. Use the appliance's normal controls to stop the cycle if it is safe to do so.
  2. Consult the appliance manual for the correct response to an interrupted supply.
  3. Do not open a machine's internal panels or attempt to alter its water-control system.
  4. Arrange inspection if the appliance leaks, displays persistent error codes or fails to resume normal operation.
When to contact a technician

Seek professional help if a washing machine repeatedly reports water-inlet errors, leaks or behaves abnormally after supply interruptions.

GUIDE 06

Device-Specific Care

Everyday habits can help keep common household electronics in better condition.

Phones & Laptops

  • Keep devices dry and out of direct sunlight.
  • Use compatible chargers and avoid damaged cables.
  • Keep software updated and back up important data.
  • Stop charging if the battery swells or the device becomes abnormally hot.

Fans & Televisions

  • Keep ventilation openings clear and follow the cleaning instructions in the manual.
  • Keep equipment away from rain, leaks and standing water.
  • Switch off equipment that makes unusual noises or smells burnt.
  • Never remove protective covers to clean internal electrical parts.

Refrigerators

  • Maintain the ventilation clearances specified by the manufacturer.
  • Keep door seals clean and check that doors close properly.
  • Limit unnecessary door opening during power cuts.
  • Follow food-safety guidance if a prolonged outage affects food temperature.

Washing Machines

  • Use the appliance according to its load and water-supply requirements.
  • Check hoses for visible external leaks or damage without dismantling the machine.
  • Keep the surrounding floor and electrical connections dry.
  • Stop using the appliance if it leaks near electrical components.
When to contact a technician

If a device repeatedly overheats, leaks, trips electrical protection, produces a burning smell or fails after exposure to water, stop using it when safe and arrange professional inspection.
